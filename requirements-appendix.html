Under ECOA and Regulation B, creditors must give the principal reasons for a covered adverse action or explain how applicants can request them. The reasons must accurately describe factors actually considered or scored. Saying that an applicant failed to reach a qualifying score is insufficient.

Regulation B §1002.9 · Official interpretation

Recommendation

External-score use should trigger explanation-provenance controls

If a lender materially relies on an external score, it should preserve the score/version used, distinguish its own model evidence from inferred upstream explanations, retain the mapping that produced any consumer-facing reason, and obtain provider-linked reason or validation evidence when the notice depends on claims about the upstream score.

Reconstruction is diagnostic evidence, not a substitute for provider-linked reasons.

Consumer-advocate takeaway

Vendor opacity should not be passed downstream to the applicant.

A specific-sounding reason should be challengeable when the creditor cannot show how that reason connects to the decision system actually used. The project does not establish that any named lender failed this standard; it identifies the evidence gap a reviewer or advocate should probe.

CFPB · Auto lending · 2025

Lenders had not validated the accuracy of their denial reasons

CFPB examiners reviewed AI/ML credit-scoring models, some with over 1,000 variables. They found that institutions had not validated whether their reason-selection processes produced accurate results and directed lenders to test those methods.

The finding concerns the same distinction examined in this project: a model can produce a prediction and an explanation without establishing that the selected denial reasons are accurate. The report does not identify SHAP or LIME as the methods involved.

Supervisory Highlights, Winter 2025, §2.1.3, pp. 7–8 · preserved report

NYDFS · Apple Card · 2021

Customers had little insight into decisions the bank could explain to examiners

Goldman could explain individual credit decisions to NYDFS, but customers experienced poor transparency and frustrating appeals over credit terms. The investigation did not establish unlawful discrimination. The case distinguishes a regulator’s access to decision evidence from a customer’s ability to understand and challenge the outcome. This study provides descriptive labels, but has not tested applicant comprehension.

Further examples: incorrect notices and an external credit assessment

CFPB, 2020: payday lenders sent incorrect principal reasons, including numerous notices caused by a coding error. The notices violated Regulation B; lenders corrected them and changed their systems. No AI method was identified. Controlled notice-mapping errors would be a separate evaluation of the translation into notice wording. Official finding, §2.3.5

Dun & Bradstreet Austria, 2025: following a mobile-contract refusal based on an automated credit assessment, the CJEU required an intelligible account of how the person’s data were actually used. This EU case concerns an outside scoring provider under GDPR, a different legal framework from US notice duties. C-203/22 judgment

CFPB Adverse Action Tech Sprint, 2020: participants explored digital notices and interactive explanations. This was a design event, not an enforcement finding. It establishes prior work on the interface; this project’s contribution must rest on the evaluated explanation problem. Official account
