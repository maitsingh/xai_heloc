# Supplementary challenge examples

Written after the main benchmark results were inspected; not part of the frozen primary evaluation. No main benchmark thresholds or review rules will change in response.

Purpose: expose limits of exact catalog checks and trusted source evidence, so favorable controlled results are not mistaken for general semantic or legal validation.

On each of the same 20 evaluation records, create two additional examples:
1. An accurate neutral paraphrase not in the approved wording catalog. The expected audit behavior is a wording flag, although the phrase is stipulated correct in this constructed example. This measures a narrow-catalog false-alarm challenge, not real consumer readability.
2. A wrong reason description entered consistently in both the notice and retained catalog, preserving matching source-feature IDs. The expected audit behavior is no discrepancy: the supplied catalog is the source of truth for the checker and it has no independent semantic oracle. This exposes a trusted-evidence blind spot.

Report these 40 challenge cases separately. These examples are same-author constructions deliberately targeting known checker limitations. They do not estimate prevalence or constitute a second held-out generalization study.
