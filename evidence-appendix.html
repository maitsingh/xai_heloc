Core claim

A downstream explanation can establish what happened in a model you can inspect. It cannot, by itself, authenticate an unavailable upstream scoring mechanism.

For an external score, the review should end in one of four states: MEASURED SUPPORTED WITH LIMITATIONS NOT ESTABLISHED or PROVIDER / LENDER RECORDS REQUIRED.

Specific principal reasons for the adverse action

Records needed. The actual decision path, scored factors, policy overrides, reason-selection method and notice wording.

Role of XAI. Signed Shapley/SHAP contributions explain a prediction relative to a reference. A reviewer must validate the reference and how selected factors become the stated reasons. An actual scorecard or rule-based model may expose its contributions directly. Estimating a supplied score does not identify its original factors.

§1002.9(b)(2) · Comments 9(b)(2)-2, -4 and -5

Choice of comparison population

Records needed. The reference-group definition, selection process, sample sizes and results under alternative choices.

Role of XAI. A reference-sensitivity experiment can show how Shapley reason selection changes when the comparison group changes. The official interpretation describes scoring comparisons with applicants near a passing score or with all applicants. A generic SHAP background is not automatically equivalent. This external-score study uses a fixed set of 24 reference records.

Comment 9(b)(2)-5
Translation from detailed factors to notice categories

Records needed. Versioned mapping rules, the factors in each category, signed effects and paired examples before and after grouping.

Role of XAI. Compare the retention and turnover of leading factors with the retention and turnover of grouped categories. The site provides both views. Independent evaluation of whether the wording accurately describes those factors remains additional work; grouping alone does not validate a notice.

Comments 9(b)(2)-2 through -4
Notification, request procedure and retained records

Records needed. Application-completion and action dates, delivery records, creditor and agency identity, the ECOA statement, and reasons or the applicable request procedure.

Role of XAI. Attribution methods do not produce these records. Workflow and notice-template checks need actual operational data. Consumer-application records are generally retained for 25 months, with longer periods in specified proceedings. Versioned models and mappings can help reconstruct the explanation used at the time.

§1002.9(a) · §1002.12
Consumer-report and credit-score disclosures

Records needed. Whether a consumer report or qualifying credit score was used, the supplier, actual score and applicable disclosure fields.

Role of XAI. Track creditor-decision reasons separately from the score’s adverse factors. FCRA disclosures do not automatically satisfy Regulation B’s specific-reasons duty. The dataset’s external-score field does not identify the original supplier or applicable notice contents.

FTC notice guidance · Comment 9(b)(2)-9
Group-level discrimination and the applicable legal framework

Records needed. Protected-group information, lending outcomes, the challenged practice, appropriate comparisons and evidence about alternatives.

Role of XAI. Subgroup evaluation can locate differences, and explanations can help inspect features. Neither supplies a complete discrimination analysis. For housing-related lending, the Fair Housing Act’s discriminatory-effects framework requires separate analysis. This study does not evaluate protected-group outcomes.

24 CFR 100.500 · Current Regulation B evaluation rules
North Carolina product requirements and third-party governance

Records needed. Lender charter, collateral, lien position, transaction terms, consumer-report use and state/federal applicability analysis. For an outside score, obtain the applicable version, input documentation, provider reasons and relevant validation evidence.

Role of XAI. Trace a displayed explanation to its evidence to investigate its accuracy. The explanation cannot establish the loan’s terms or legal scope. Current interagency model-risk guidance discusses proprietary vendor products; it does not prescribe a single explanation method.

NC G.S. 75-1.1 · NC Chapter 24 · Model-risk guidance, §VII

Shapley and custom LIME-style calculations were run in this study. The other methods below are possible additions, not reported experiments.

Shapley / SHAP: contributions relative to a comparison

Allocates a prediction’s difference from a reference across features. Reference sensitivity and individual-versus-grouped contributions can investigate reason-selection choices.

Evaluate: reference justification, feature dependence, signed aggregation and the connection to actual scored factors. Attribution of a supplied score leaves that score’s original calculation unexplained.

Nair and colleagues: adverse-action Shapley decomposition
LIME: a simple approximation near one record

Fits an interpretable local model around a prediction. Separate generated records and repeated random seeds can assess local fidelity and repeatability.

Evaluate: approximation error at the record, held-out local fit, sampling variability and whether generated combinations respect credit-feature relationships. A good local fit cannot establish the original reasons of an unknown upstream scorer.

Ribeiro, Singh and Guestrin: LIME
Interpretable scorecards, additive models and decision rules

Expose the contributions or rules of the model actually used. A separately evaluated interpretable alternative could establish whether comparable predictive performance is achievable with a more inspectable design.

Evaluate: predictive quality, actual rule execution and the wording of the resulting reasons. A transparent substitute explains the substitute, not the original complex model.

HELOC interpretable-model example
Counterfactual explanations / DiCE: changes that reverse a decision

Search for alternative inputs that change the specified model’s output. They can support a separate investigation of feasible recourse.

Evaluate: decision reversal, immutable fields, costs, feasible changes, stability and the full lending policy. A path to approval answers a different question from the principal reasons for the original refusal.

DiCE documentation
Anchors: local conditions that preserve a prediction

Finds rule-style conditions under which a prediction remains the same with high precision under a defined sampling distribution. It can supplement ranked feature explanations.

Evaluate: precision, coverage and the relevance of the perturbation distribution. An anchor is a local behavioral rule; it does not recover an unknown provider’s internal mechanism.
