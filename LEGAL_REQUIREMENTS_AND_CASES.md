# Credit explanations: requirements, evidence and documented failures

Source status checked 18 October 2026. The legal analysis concerns a proposed US consumer-credit use, with a North Carolina home equity line of credit (HELOC) as the jurisdiction/product example. The dataset does not identify a real creditor, application decision, notice or external-score provider. European cases are comparisons under different law.

## The review question

Can the creditor substantiate that the disclosed principal reasons describe the factors used in the applicant's actual adverse action? This requires the decision process, the reason-selection process and the delivered notice. Explaining a repayment-risk prediction supplies only part of that evidence.

There is no general regulatory SHAP score, LIME fidelity score, prediction AUC or reason-retention percentage that makes a credit system compliant. Numerical tolerances in this study are research comparisons. A reason can be stable and wrong. Different reasonable explanations do not, by themselves, prove that a notice violates the law.

## Definitions and scope

- **Adverse action, Regulation B:** includes refusing requested credit, specified unfavorable account changes or terminations, and refusing an increase. Accepted counteroffers and the exclusions in 12 CFR 1002.2(c) matter. A high risk prediction alone is not an adverse action.
- **Principal reasons:** the main reasons for the action, not every important input across the model's population. The official interpretation requires disclosed reasons to correspond to factors actually considered or scored. It addresses scoring, judgmental review, combined processes and automatic-denial rules separately.
- **Credit score, FCRA:** the statutory definition is in 15 USC 1681g(f)(2)(A). It excludes certain mortgage automated-underwriting ratings and other underwriting elements. A column named "ExternalRiskEstimate" does not establish the supplier's identity, consumer-report status or every statutory trigger.
- **Fair lending:** whether a practice unlawfully discriminates is a separate question from whether a particular notice gives accurate reasons. Explaining a decision does not establish nondiscrimination. This study does not measure protected-group outcomes.

Sources: [Regulation B definitions](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1002/2/); [official notice interpretation](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1002/interp-9/); [FTC compilation of FCRA, sections 609 and 615](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/fcra-may2023-508.pdf).

## Binding US duties and the evidence a reviewer would need

| Duty and trigger | What the reviewer needs | What this project contributes; what remains missing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Specific principal reasons under 12 CFR 1002.9(b)(2), when reasons are provided for covered adverse action | The actual inputs, scoring and policy rules, overrides and human review; a validated way of selecting principal factors; wording that describes those factors | Shapley attribution explains our research Random Forest relative to a defined reference. Score-estimation accuracy does not authenticate the original score's factors. Actual lender decision rules and delivered reasons are missing. |
| Reasons from the relevant decision component, official comments 9(b)(2)-6 through -8 | Which scoring stage, judgmental assessment or automatic-denial rule led to refusal | A credit-risk attribution cannot identify a separate affordability, collateral or policy rule that actually caused denial. Request the complete decision record rather than explain the risk model alone. |
| Timely notification and required content, 1002.9(a) | Completed-application and action dates, creditor identity and address, applicable federal enforcement agency, ECOA notice, reasons or the required request procedure; delivery records | Not measured by the public credit data. For a standard completed consumer application, notification is generally due within 30 days. If the request route is used, an applicant has 60 days to request reasons and the creditor must provide the statement within 30 days of that request. Exceptions and other application/account situations require separate analysis. |
| Retention, 1002.12(b) | Evaluated application information, action notice and reasons, and relevant written applicant complaints | Generally 25 months for consumer applications, with extensions for investigations/proceedings. Retaining model, preprocessing, reason-mapping and explanation versions is a proposed engineering control supporting reconstruction; the rule does not specify a universal ML log schema. Our reproducible research files are not a lender's retention system. |
| FCRA 1681m(a), adverse action based wholly or partly on a consumer report | Report supplier contact information; notice that the reporting agency did not make the decision; free-report and dispute rights; evidence of actual report use | Identify the source and role of each input. The dataset supplies neither the reporting agency nor the required notice/delivery information. |
| Additional FCRA disclosures when a numerical credit score is used in the covered action | Actual score, possible range, date, source and key adverse score factors, subject to statutory limits | Score factors and the creditor's principal denial reasons must be kept distinct. One disclosure does not automatically satisfy the other. Do not substitute reasons from our estimated score for the original provider's factors. |
| HELOC-specific and property-valuation duties, where triggered | Product terms, lien position, collateral and valuations, application/disclosure records | Regulation Z 1026.40 governs home-equity-plan disclosures and restrictions. Regulation B 1002.14 concerns copies of appraisals and other written valuations for first-lien dwelling applications. None can be established from a SHAP plot. |

Sources: [1002.9](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1002/9/), [interpretation](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1002/interp-9/), [1002.12](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1002/12/), [FCRA text](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/fcra-may2023-508.pdf), [FTC notice explanation](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/using-consumer-reports-credit-decisions-what-know-about-adverse-action-risk-based-pricing-notices), [Regulation Z 1026.40](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/40/), [1002.14](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1002/14/).

### What should not become an invented legal test

The official interpretation does not require precisely four ECOA reasons, a directional adjective, a guaranteed path to approval, a causal explanation or one specified XAI method. The FCRA score-factor limits are a separate matter. SHAP's mathematical consistency property is not a promise that rankings will remain unchanged when the applicant, comparison population or explanation definition changes. No primary source reviewed establishes a general regulator preference for SHAP over LIME. Do not hide an actually used suspect proxy by renaming it as a more acceptable feature: investigate its use and describe actual factors accurately.

### Current source status

CFPB Circulars 2022-03 and 2023-03 were withdrawn on 12 May 2025. They remain relevant historical explanations of the Bureau's concern about complex models and notices, but should be labeled withdrawn. Current legal conclusions here rely on Regulation B and its official interpretation. [CFPB withdrawal list](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/compliance/guidance/withdrawn-guidance/).

### Accountability and liability

Regulation B 1002.16 identifies administrative enforcement and potential civil remedies, including actual damages, specified punitive damages, equitable relief and attorney fees under ECOA. The relevant regulator depends on the creditor. An explanation diagnostic is evidence for review, not immunity from those duties. Liability depends on the claim, facts and applicable defenses; vendor involvement does not turn a prediction-quality metric into a defense. The commentary also contains a specific defense for certain third-party notice failures when the creditor supplied timely accurate information and maintained reasonable preventive procedures. [1002.16](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1002/16/), [comment 9(g)-3](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1002/interp-9/).

## Supervisory model governance and third-party opacity

The April 2026 interagency revised model-risk guidance, issued by the Federal Reserve as **SR 26-2**, supersedes SR 11-7. The Fed identifies it as most relevant to its regulated banking organizations above $30 billion in assets. It is supervisory guidance, distinct from the binding notice duties. Its risk-based principles address model development and use, validation, monitoring and governance. Section VII explicitly addresses vendor data, parameters and models: proprietary components can restrict access, but model-risk principles still apply.

Useful evidence includes development and performance documentation, intended-use limits, testing on the institution's population, independent challenge proportionate to risk, change controls, monitoring and a responsible owner. For an external score, request the applicable score version, input definitions and provenance, provider reason codes and their meanings, validation information or access, and change notifications. This is a proposed evidence request informed by the guidance; it is not a statutory demand to publish all vendor weights.

Sources: [SR 26-2 letter and applicability](https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/SR2602.htm), [guidance including vendor products, section VII](https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/SR2602a1.pdf).

## North Carolina: scope before conclusions

For a North Carolina HELOC review, establish the creditor's charter/regulator, borrower and property location, consumer purpose, first or junior lien, line limit, rates and fees, and whether this is an initial refusal or a later restriction. NC G.S. 41A-4(b1) addresses discrimination in residential-real-estate-related lending. G.S. 75-1.1 concerns unfair or deceptive practices in commerce; a particular claim requires its elements and applicable defenses. G.S. 24-1.1E restricts defined high-cost home loans, including qualifying open-end plans. Its amount, security and pricing conditions cannot be evaluated from these credit attributes. These laws do not supply a SHAP acceptance threshold.

Sources: [41A-4](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_41A/GS_41A-4.html), [75-1.1](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_75/GS_75-1.1.html), [24-1.1E](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_24/GS_24-1.1E.html).

## Documented cases: distinguish findings, governance disputes and comparisons

1. **CFPB, Winter 2025, Advanced Technologies Special Edition, issue 38, section 2.1.3, pp. 7–8.** Examiners reviewed auto lenders using AI/ML scoring, in some cases with over 1,000 variables. They found insufficient assurance of notice compliance and no validation that reason-selection processes produced accurate results. Institutions were directed to test and validate their methods. The report does not identify the lenders or name SHAP or LIME. This is direct evidence of a real explanation-validation problem, not a finding that one XAI method is unlawful. The Bureau's original PDF link currently returns 404; the linked copy reproduces the Bureau's report. [CFPB report, preserved copy](https://business.cch.com/BFLD/CFPB-Supervisory-Highlights-Advanced-Technologies-Special-Edition-Issue-38-Winter-2025-01172025.pdf).
2. **CFPB, Winter 2020, issue 21, section 2.3.5, p. 9.** Incorrect principal reasons in payday-lending notices violated Regulation B. A coding error caused numerous incorrect notices; lenders corrected notices and changed generation systems. The report does not identify an AI method. Implication: validate the delivered reason-generation process, not just numerical attributions. [Official report](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_supervisory-highlights_issue-21_2020-02.pdf).
3. **NYDFS, Apple Card investigation, March 2021, pp. 7–8.** Goldman could explain individual decisions to the regulator, but applicants experienced poor transparency and frustrating appeals over credit terms. The investigation did not establish unlawful discrimination. Distinguish these communication findings from an adverse-action-notice violation; not every granted-credit term triggers denial-notice duties. [Official report](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2021/03/rpt_202103_apple_card_investigation.pdf).
4. **CFPB/Upstart, June 2022.** The no-action letter ended at Upstart's request after proposed model changes required agency review time. This documents the governance significance of changing proprietary models. It is not a finding that Upstart's explanation method failed or that the platform was banned. [Official account](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/newsroom/cfpb-issues-order-to-terminate-upstart-no-action-letter/).
5. **CJEU, Dun & Bradstreet Austria, C-203/22, 27 February 2025.** A mobile-contract refusal relied on an automated credit assessment. Applying GDPR, the Court required an intelligible account of how the person's data were actually used; an algorithm alone is insufficient. Input-variation examples may help. Claims of trade secrecy require balancing through the competent authority/court, rather than categorical refusal. This is a separate EU standard, especially relevant to the problem of an outside scoring provider. [Judgment, paragraphs 58–66 and operative part](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:62022CJ0203).
6. **CJEU, SCHUFA, C-634/21, 7 December 2023.** An external score can itself fall within GDPR Article 22's automated-decision rules when the lender gives it a determining role. The case does not ban all scoring. [Court account](https://curia.europa.eu/jcms/upload/docs/application/pdf/2023-12/cp230186en.pdf).

## Actual practice and empirical evidence

Upstart publicly reports using SHAP to generate reasons for AI-model declines, aggregating related features and checking directional wording and missing inputs. This is provider-reported practice, not an independent regulatory finding that every notice complies. Its page also discusses planned semantic clustering and LLM-assisted drafting; do not treat the stated rollout plan as independently verified deployment. [Upstart](https://www.upstart.com/lenders/regulatory-compliance/adverse-action-notices/).

FinRegLab's 2023 empirical research tested industry and open-source tools using consumer credit data. Models included logistic regression, neural networks, gradient boosting and a company-submitted Random Forest. These are research implementations of model families relevant to lending, not proof that our nine-input forest matches a production lender. Signed adverse SHAP rankings performed well on its fidelity tests for complex models, but the project did not validate actual consumer-facing notices or certify compliance. Features were masked, limiting semantic review. [Empirical report, sections 3.4 and 4](https://finreglab.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/FinRegLab_2023-07-13_Empirical-White-Paper_Explainability-and-Fairness_Insights-from-Consumer-Lending.pdf).

## Which method supplies which missing evidence?

| Question | Useful method/control | Remaining limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Which inputs contributed to this model's prediction relative to a stated comparison? | Signed Shapley/SHAP, with justified comparison records and dependency/grouping checks | An additive decomposition is not causal proof, a complete decision trace or an explanation of an unknown provider's score formula. |
| Does a simpler explanation track the model near this applicant? | LIME with independently evaluated local error, plausible generated records and repeatability testing | Fidelity depends on the sampled neighborhood. A good average fit can hide poor fit for the specific record or a subgroup. |
| What did the actual transparent model do? | Direct decomposition of a deployed scorecard/additive model or execution of its actual rules | Training a new transparent substitute explains the substitute, not the original complex model. |
| What feasible changes would reverse the decision? | Constrained counterfactuals, checked against the complete current decision process | Recourse differs from original principal reasons; correlated fields, time, costs and changes in policy affect feasibility. |
| How was the outside score actually produced? | Provider's applicable input/reason documentation, validation access and records tied to the score version | Better predictive reconstruction cannot authenticate the original inputs or logic. Additional XAI on our approximation explains our approximation. |
| Was the right notice produced and retained? | Decision replay, versioned reason mappings, notice/delivery records and retention controls | These are operational evidence, not outputs obtainable from SHAP/LIME. |

Method sources: [SHAP](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.07874), [LIME](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.04938), [alternative Shapley definitions](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08474), [DiCE](https://interpret.ml/DiCE/readme.html). Controls and the proposed use of diagnostics above are this project's analysis, not a regulatory prescription.

## Comparative law without importing it into the US assessment

For a separately scoped EU deployment, AI Act Articles 12–14 address records, deployer information and oversight; Article 86 concerns explanations for specified consequential decisions. Roles, high-risk classification, exceptions and transition dates must be established. The Commission's July 2026 timetable states 2 December 2027 for Annex III high-risk rules. GDPR obligations already require their own analysis. [Article 13](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-13), [Article 86](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-86), [amended timetable](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/ai-omnibus-enters-force).

New York S8115C is a proposed automated-lending assessment bill, still in Senate committee when checked, not an enacted obligation for this experiment. [Bill status](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S8115/amendment/C). Do not import employment bias-audit law or insurance circulars as credit-notice requirements.

## What conclusion can the workbench support?

It can document measured fit and explanation behavior, identify the precise claim an estimated score does not substantiate, and specify the evidence needed to review that claim. It cannot resolve the original score's actual reasons, a real lender's complete decision or the adequacy of a delivered notice without those records. The useful governance output is a reasoned, source-linked conclusion about evidence sufficiency for a particular claim—not a percentage labeled legal compliance.
